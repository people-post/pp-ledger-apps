PP Ledger — who does what

An overview for stakeholders: what the chain is, who holds which powers, and where each power stops. As of pp-ledger 1.5.0 and pp-ledger-apps, October 2026.

The chain in one paragraph

PP Ledger is an account-based chain with a native token and tokens issued by approved organisations. Every account is kept alive by small, regular renewal fees. Blocks are produced by registered miners, and a beacon signs the chain's progress. Every important power belongs to a set of keys — several people, a threshold of whom must sign — rather than to a person or a server. Each power is limited by rules every node enforces.

Shared keysPowers need several signatures (for example 2 of 3), collected as operation files, signed offline if wished.
Rules, not trustEvery node checks every rule; a key holder cannot exceed their role, and every act is on chain.
Verify, don't believeWallets prove balances, keys and names back to the chain's first block; no server has to be trusted.
A way backLost or stolen keys can be replaced: by the recovery set for the system, and by an optional recovery set for any account.

How value flows: the admin set mints new supply only into the reserve (within a per-epoch cap) → the reserve funds the registrar and others → the registrar creates and funds issuers; anyone funded creates user accounts → every fee goes to the fee account, which pays relays and miners in periodic rounds → an account that cannot pay its renewal ends, and what it held goes to the recycle account.

The roles

Admin set (the genesis account)

The chain's governors: 3 or more keys, 2 or more to sign.
Can
  • Mint new native tokens into the reserve, up to the cap per epoch.
  • Change the chain's settings (fees, limits, miner rules), effective from the next epoch.
  • Certify the next beacon generation (moving or replacing the beacon).
Cannot
  • Touch any account's funds, create accounts, or send anywhere but the reserve.
  • Change the recovery set; act at all while a recovery of the admin set is announced.
Apps: Admin, Signer

Recovery set (system)

Cold keys, kept apart from the admins; used almost never.
Can
  • Name a new admin set if the admins' keys are lost or stolen. The admin set is frozen during the delay (about a month by default), then replaced.
Cannot
  • Mint, spend, or change settings; hold admin keys itself.
  • Act without its own threshold of signatures.
Apps: Genesis, Signer

Reserve

The treasury: holds the supply not yet in circulation.
Can
  • Transfer to any account (fund the registrar, programmes, partners); create user accounts.
Cannot
  • Mint (only the admin set adds to it); create issuer accounts; act below its threshold.
Apps: Reserve, Signer

Registrar

The gatekeeper for issuers and their names.
Can
  • Create issuer accounts for an organisation's keys, and fund them.
  • Give an issuer its symbol (4–7 letters, e.g. ACME): its token's ticker and its domain, once and for good.
  • Decide whether a symbol freed by an ended issuer is given again, and to whom.
Cannot
  • Rename or take back a symbol while its account lives; control an issuer after creating it.
  • Give 1–3 letter names: those are the system accounts', fixed at genesis.
Apps: Registrar, Signer

Fee account and its distributors

Collects every fee; pays those who run the network.
Can
  • Run periodic fee rounds: gather the period's chain activity, apply the round's rules, and pay relays and miners in one signed batch, each payout marked to its round.
Cannot
  • Collect anything but fees, or pay without its holders' threshold.
  • Hide a round: its payouts and evidence are on chain for the next round to check.
Apps: Fees, Signer

Beacon operator

Runs the node that anchors the chain's progress.
Can
  • Sign the chain's latest block so wallets can verify it, and serve the chain to others.
Cannot
  • Hold or use any account's keys, or change balances or rules.
  • Replace itself: only the admin set certifies a new beacon.
Tools: pp-beacon

Miners

Produce blocks; registered accounts with a stake.
Can
  • Register (with a stake and a fee) and produce blocks when elected for a slot.
  • Be paid in fee rounds for the blocks they produced.
Cannot
  • Change rules or include invalid transactions: every node rejects such blocks.
  • Stay registered while missing their slots (they are deregistered automatically).
Tools: pp-miner

Relay operators

The network's public front: serve wallets and apps.
Can
  • Pass transactions on and answer queries; be paid in fee rounds for the service measured.
Cannot
  • Forge a balance, a key or a name: wallets check every answer against the chain's proofs.
Tools: pp-relay

Token issuers

Organisations with an issuer account (e.g. shares of a company), run by several key holders.
Can
  • Issue their own token, under their symbol, to holders; send native or other tokens.
  • Replace their signers; name a recovery set for their account.
  • See their supply out, their balance and what renewals cost.
Cannot
  • Issue anyone else's token, or choose or change their own symbol (the registrar gives it).
  • Skip renewals: an issuer that cannot pay ends, and its symbol becomes free.
Apps: Token, Signer

Users

Anyone with an account: people, shops, services.
Can
  • Hold, send and receive the native token and issued tokens, to a number or a symbol.
  • See everything proven, in the last-resort wallet; move funds away from leaked keys at once.
  • Name a recovery set (friends, or a backup key) as a way back.
Cannot
  • Have a symbol or issue tokens; send more than they hold.
  • Keep an account that cannot pay its renewal (its balance goes to recycling).
Apps: Wallet (and plugins such as pp-browser)

Recovery-key holders (for an account)

People or places an account's owner chose as its way back.
Can
  • Give the account new keys if its own are lost or stolen. The account is frozen immediately (a thief's key signs nothing), and the new keys take over after the delay chosen.
  • Withdraw that, change or remove the set, at their own threshold.
Cannot
  • Spend from the account directly, or act below their threshold.
  • Act silently: the announcement is on chain and shown in the owner's wallet.
Apps: Wallet (Recovery)

The genesis ceremony

Once, at the start: the founding key holders together.
Decides
  • Who holds each system account and the recovery set, and how many must sign.
  • The beacon's first key, the first miners, the starting settings, and the system names (e.g. PPL for the native token).
Fixed afterwards
  • The network's identity, slot timing and system names; system accounts are created only here.
Apps: Genesis, Signer
Open decisions
  • System names: PPL, RSV, REG, FEE and RCY are provisional, pending agreement; they are fixed at the genesis of the live network.
  • Ended accounts: whether an ended account's number and symbol may be reused, and after how long, is to be decided as one rule.
  • Domain resolution: symbols are this chain's names, not internet domains. How they will resolve (a suffix we own, or natively in pp-browser) is not decided yet.
